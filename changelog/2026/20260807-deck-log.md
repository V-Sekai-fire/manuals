## The zone tier, recorded

`zone-server-h2o` was archived and is unarchived again. It is the host repository, and
[RFD 0107](https://v-sekai-multiplayer-fabric.github.io/multiplayer-fabric-manuals/rfd/0107-janet-scripting-over-a-c-taskweft-core/)
records the tier around it: h2o as HTTP ingress and reverse proxy, Janet as the dynamic layer for routing glue and
configuration, `libtaskweft` as a C library holding HTN search and game state, and Godot as the client over GDExtension.
Janet loads the C core as a native module. Rust is out, so the core is C.

FoundationDB stays the store, through `libfdb_c`. `rfd/0075` stands and `rfd/0006`'s CockroachDB does not describe this
tier, so the FDB record set stays in force: `rfd/0002`, `rfd/0073`, `rfd/0074`, `rfd/0084`, `rfd/0097`, and `rfd/0103`.
`ecto_foundationdb` and `ecto-bench-tpcc` keep a live consumer.

The record states its own gaps rather than guessing past them. The startup, memory and speed claims for this tier are not
measured, and `data/measurements/` holds nothing for Janet or a C taskweft core. Four questions stay open: which part
drives the `libfdb_c` callbacks, where Elixir and `zone-backend` sit, whether Janet replaces the libriscv guest runtime,
and which part terminates a WebTransport session.

## The guest boundary, recorded

[RFD 0108](https://v-sekai-multiplayer-fabric.github.io/multiplayer-fabric-manuals/rfd/0108-local-cabi-guests-and-in-tick-fanout/)
makes a simulation guest a local shared object loaded over the plain C ABI, and h2o routes to it with no network hop. The
CDN keeps one job, which is bulk asset delivery, and it no longer delivers guest programs for simulation.

Fan-out runs inside the tick. One publish ring per zone, one writer, and many readers holding cursors. The 100-byte packet
of `rfd/0053` is serialized once and fanned out as a pointer and a refcount, so N subscribers cost one serialization
rather than N. A cross-loop delivery takes one batched wakeup per tick, which caps a loop near 64 wakeups per second at
64 Hz.

FoundationDB stays off that path. `rfd/0097` measured a watch fire at 12214.3 us, which is 78 percent of a 15.6 ms tick,
against 8.9 us for an `AF_UNIX` round trip. The watch probe also failed at exactly 10000 watches with error 1032, and a
watch is edge-triggered, so it loses values between the fire and the re-read. The tick owns the `libfdb_c` callback chain
of `rfd/0073` and appends the durable log off-tick.

The record states the cost of local mode rather than hiding it. A local `.so` shares the zone's address space, so a guest
fault takes the zone down. `rfd/0083` chose Fil-C for memory safety and `rfd/0095` built two guest classes for the same
reason, and local mode has neither property. That suits first-party simulation code and does not suit user-generated
code, so `rfd/0094`'s UGC loop needs its own record.

RFD 0108 closes two of RFD 0107's four open questions, which are the database path owner and the guest boundary. Two stay
open and one arrives: where Elixir and `zone-backend` sit, which part terminates a WebTransport session, and how a UGC
guest runs.

## Two tiers, FoundationDB, and a cost floor

[RFD 0109](https://v-sekai-multiplayer-fabric.github.io/multiplayer-fabric-manuals/rfd/0109-two-tiers-fdb-and-the-cost-floor/)
names 276.48 USD per month as the cost floor. That is 1000 concurrent players at 4 hours per day, from `rfd/0104`. It is
table stakes rather than a ceiling, and the design assumes it. The same numbers give a peak of 256 Mbps sustained
upstream and 13824 GB per month, which is 13.5 TiB. The 15 USD budget of `rfd/0102` bought 642 GB, or 0.19 hours per day
for each of 1000 users, so it never reached the target.

The tier count drops to two. Godot is the client, holding rendering, XR, IK, and local physics. Elixir and Phoenix form
the server, holding identity, the room directory, and the presence relay. The server relays presence rather than
simulating it, so Phoenix supplies the identity and sessions that a Godot server would need written in GDScript.

The store stays FoundationDB, through `ecto_foundationdb`, so `rfd/0075` stands. The record accepts `rfd/0103`'s limits
rather than working around them: no joins, no `or`, no aggregates in the database, and one Between clause per query. That
trade holds because the relational need is analytical, and the analytical half runs on zstd Parquet and DuckDB as
`data/measurements/` already does. OLTP access is point lookups by player and room identity.

Twelve stores are recorded with their outcome. TiDB, YDB, and Apache Ignite are viable and not chosen, and the record
says why for each. TiDB is the one that keeps relational form with no adapter work, at the cost of more processes and
MySQL's weaker constraint set. Rust was banned and then unbanned during the search, which returned TiDB to the list.
Rivet 2.0 is Apache 2.0 and ships a TypeScript SDK, with no Elixir or Godot client.

`taskweft` stays an Elixir NIF, which `V-Sekai-fire/multiplayer-fabric-taskweft` already is, so Janet and the C
`libtaskweft` are dropped.

`rfd/0107` and `rfd/0108` are marked abandoned and carry a pointer to this record. Their measurements stay valid, and the
back-pressure rule of `rfd/0108` still applies to any relay.

Five questions stay open on record: player-hosted zones against server-hosted, interest management as the only lever on
the 256 kbps figure, server authority that a relay cannot give, whether the Godot WebTransport client speaks to a Phoenix
endpoint, and how many FoundationDB processes run and where.

## The zone becomes one pinned Godot build, hosted as a Rivet actor

[RFD 0110](https://v-sekai-multiplayer-fabric.github.io/multiplayer-fabric-manuals/rfd/0110-h2o-libriscv-as-a-rivet-actor/)
makes a zone a child process under Rivet's `container-runner`, one process per room. The child is the engine at tag
`v2026.06.27.1907-multiplayer-fabric`, which resolves to commit `2cecde75` and reports Godot 4.7.0 beta. That tag's
`modules/` already carries `sandbox` for libriscv guests, `http3` for WebTransport, `multiplayer_fabric` and
`multiplayer_fabric_asset` for the zone, `xr_grid` for presence, and `openxr` with `webxr` for XR. So one build is the
client, the zone server, and the guest host.

A C child of h2o plus libriscv stays possible and is not built. Its only advantage is memory per process, and that figure
is unmeasured. No `resonance` module appears at the tag, so `rfd/0022`'s spatial audio needs checking before anyone relies
on it.

One process per room deletes work rather than adding it. `rfd/0072`, `rfd/0082`, and `rfd/0086` become unnecessary,
because each exists only to hold many zones in one process. `rfd/0080` narrows to one room's entities. A guest fault kills
one room rather than every room, which is what `rfd/0095` wanted from Bubblewrap.

The actor input is four optional CBOR fields, read from `container-runner/src/input.rs`: `command`, `args`, `env`, and
`port`. The decoded input is also the actor's persisted state, so a woken actor restores the same launch spec, and unknown
fields are ignored rather than rejected. A room's scene and its tuning therefore travel in the actor input, and no
zone-side registry is needed.

The transport is settled by the runner's source rather than by its documentation. `container-runner` is TCP-only.
`src/proxy.rs` forwards to `http://127.0.0.1:{child_port}` and the `ws://` form, `src/child.rs` checks readiness with
`TcpStream::connect((Ipv4Addr::LOCALHOST, child_port))`, and no `udp`, `quic`, `http3`, or `h2` appears in the runner
source. `TCP_NODELAY` does not appear either, so Nagle is unmitigated there.

The answer is two sockets in one child. A TCP port satisfies Rivet's contract and carries the control plane. A UDP port
carries game traffic outside Rivet's tunnel, with the engine's `http3` picoquic module on it. Rivet never touches the pose
stream, so `rfd/0008`, `rfd/0023`, `rfd/0049`, `rfd/0058`, and `rfd/0088` all stand. The cost is that Rivet's gateway does
not route the UDP port, so a zone directory must publish the host and port, which makes the Uro role required rather than
optional.
